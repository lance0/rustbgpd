# Single-owner RIB actor ceiling at 1M routes — 2026-07

Does the single-threaded `RibManager` actor block past the `/readyz`
200 ms core-readiness deadline during a policy reload at
internet-table scale? This is the measurement that gates the biggest
pending architectural decision — ADR-0100 (parallel-RibManager
sharding). ADR-0105 discloses that the grouped export-policy transition
runs **two un-chunked O(table) snapshot polls** plus an O(outbound-peers
+ table × dirty-peers) finalize tail inside the single-owner actor, and
warns that at ~1M routes a single such poll plausibly exceeds the 200 ms
readiness deadline, depooling the node during a *legitimate* reload
(LAN-418). This receipt confirms or refutes that empirically.

> **Question: at ~1M routes, does a single policy-transition actor poll
> exceed the 200 ms `/readyz` deadline, and if the node depools during a
> reload, what actually causes it?**

**Commit measured:** `fbc840d5` (main; v0.51.0 tag plus post-tag
outbound-correctness work). Real daemon, real BGP sessions, release
build. Box: 64-core / 128 GiB.

## Fleet shape (disclosed)

- **500 route-server clients × 2,000 IPv4 /24s each = 1,000,000 routes**
  in Loc-RIB; each client's Adj-RIB-Out is the full table minus its own
  slice (998,000 prefixes).
- **300 of 500 peers** change export policy on each reload (a realistic
  partial-fleet policy change, to load the finalize tail and the
  re-advertisement flush).
- **3 SIGHUP policy reloads** (member-out community swap gen-a↔gen-b),
  each triggered directly against the converged daemon.
- Scenario generated by `bench/scale/reloadstall/gen-scenario.py`
  (`prometheus_addr` exposes the actor-poll histogram + `/readyz`).

## Headline

| Metric (1,000,000 routes, 500 clients, 300 changed, 3 reloads) | Value |
|---|---|
| **Worst single policy-transition actor poll** (any `poll_kind`, any reload) | **0.1–0.2 s** (under the 200 ms deadline) |
| `prefix_snapshot` polls (the two O(table) scans) | 6 total; mean 59 ms; worst bucket 0.1–0.2 s |
| `finalize` polls (the tail) | 3 total; mean 109 ms; worst bucket 0.1–0.2 s |
| `bounded` cooperative polls | 9,807; mean 0.7 ms; all < 100 ms |
| `/readyz` latency **during** the transition (`in_progress=1`) | **1–3 ms** (responsive throughout ~2.6 s) |
| Transition wall time (SIGHUP → `in_progress` clears) | 2,618 / ~2,560 / 2,675 ms |
| **`/readyz` at commit → flush boundary** | R1: **200 @ 2.40 s** · R2: **503 @ 2.02 s** · R3: **503 @ 1.64 s** then 200 @ 0.46 s |
| Re-advertisement flush after each commit | ~300k UPDATEs (300 peers × 998k routes), spanning ~2.7–5.0 s post-commit |
| Peak daemon RSS | 3,046 MiB |

## Verdict

**The policy-transition snapshot polls do NOT blow the deadline at 1M —
the ADR-0105 concern is refuted for the transition itself.** Every one
of the 6 `prefix_snapshot`, 3 `finalize`, and 9,807 `bounded` polls
across three reloads completed in ≤ 200 ms (worst case 0.1–0.2 s). The
transition yields cooperatively; `/readyz` answered in 1–3 ms for the
entire ~2.6 s each transition was in progress.

**But the node still depools on every reload — the cause is the
post-commit outbound re-advertisement flush, not the transition.**
Immediately after `in_progress` clears, the actor flushes the new
Adj-RIB-Out to the 300 changed peers (~300k UPDATEs; the sent-counter
surges +300k in exactly this window), and this blocks the runtime for
~1.6–2.4 s. `/readyz` returned 503 (R2, R3) or a late 200 (R1) in that
window in all three reloads. The depool is real and reproducible; it
just lives in the distribution path, not the policy-transition path.

### What this means for ADR-0100

The measurement argues **against** committing to RibManager sharding
now. Sharding would parallelize the grouped policy transition — but the
transition is not the ceiling at 1M; its polls stay under the readiness
deadline. The actual depool driver is the outbound flush, which is
addressable by making distribution **cooperative** (yield to the reactor
periodically / chunk the flush) — a far smaller change than sharding.
Recommendation: pursue the cooperative-flush fix and re-measure before
reconsidering ADR-0100.

## Secondary findings

1. **`/readyz` does not fail fast at its 200 ms deadline.**
   `CORE_READINESS_DEADLINE = 200 ms` bounds an internal wait, not the
   HTTP probe response: the probe returned a late 200 after 2.40 s (R1)
   and a 503 only after 1.64–2.02 s (R2/R3). A health-checker sees a
   multi-second hang rather than a prompt 503. (Filed separately.)

2. **The `reloadstall` harness completion-detection wedges at 1M.** The
   daemon converged the full 500-client fan-out correctly
   (`bgp_rib_adj_out_prefixes = 998,000` for every peer, uniform 1,498
   correctly-chunked UPDATEs each, Send-Q drained to zero), but the
   harness's per-observer completion bitmap never reached 100% and it
   parked indefinitely. It was validated at 700 × 400k, not at 1M. This
   receipt therefore drove the three reloads by SIGHUP directly against
   the converged daemon rather than through the harness's reload loop.
   (Filed separately; not a daemon fault.)

## Honesty notes

- **Three reloads, not a large sample.** The per-`poll_kind` counts are
  small (6 snapshot, 3 finalize). The claim "no transition poll exceeded
  200 ms" is a max over three reloads, not a p99.9 over hundreds. It is
  strong enough to refute the sharp "a single snapshot poll blows the
  deadline" hypothesis, and the depool finding reproduced 3/3.
- **Reloads driven by direct SIGHUP**, not the harness's churn-and-reload
  loop, because of finding 2 above. SIGHUP + live-file swap is exactly
  the production reload path; the transition and flush are the daemon's
  real code, unmodified. What is *not* measured here is receiver-side
  UPDATE-delivery stall under concurrent churn (the reload-stall receipt
  covers that at 400k).
- **`/readyz` sampling cadence ~195 ms.** The stall durations are the
  blocking `/readyz` request's own latency (a probe issued into the stall
  blocks until the runtime frees), so the reported 1.6–2.4 s are direct
  measurements, not interpolations.
